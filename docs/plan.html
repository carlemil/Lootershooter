Locked decisions

Four questions were put to the owner and answered; everything else follows from them. First-person only. GDScript. Scripted bots that tune themselves from recorded player data. Money that lives and dies inside one match.

AreaDecision
EngineGodot 4.4+ (latest stable at scaffold time), GDScript. One project, three export presets: client-windows, server-linux and server-windows. Both server presets use Dedicated Server export mode and run with --headless; Docker ships the Linux one, the Windows one runs a match server on a Windows box without Docker.
NetcodeENet through MultiplayerAPI. Server-authoritative. Server tick 30 Hz, client 60+ Hz. Client-side prediction with reconciliation for your own body, interpolation for everyone else, lag-compensated hit registration on the server.
HostingOne container is one match instance: up to 20 slots, looping lobby → 15-minute match → results. Scale by running more containers. docker compose for local development.
Server browserA registry service of about sixty lines of Python standard library. Game servers heartbeat every 10 s; clients fetch /servers. In-game list shows name, map, players, ping and mode, plus direct connect by IP.
PerspectiveFirst-person only. Arms and weapon viewmodel for yourself, a full-body model for everyone else.
Players2–20 humans; bots fill to a configurable target (default 20). Solo, duo, trio, squad.
MatchNo hard cap. At 15:00 the circle has closed to 0 m. The match then runs on for the seconds it takes the second-to-last team to bleed out from the outside-zone damage ramp. Last team standing wins. Cash on hand is a scoreboard stat, never a tiebreak. Death is final for the round: no respawns, you spectate until it ends. Knocked is not dead; a teammate can still revive you. Anyone who joins mid-match spectates and plays the next one.
ZoneNo phases. The safe circle shrinks continuously from 1200 m to zero at 15:00. Its center wanders along a smooth seeded noise path at up to 1.5 m/s, shown on the minimap 30 s ahead. Outside: cash drains, and health damage ramps with both the time you have spent outside and how far out you are, capped at 100% of your health per second: 60 s at the edge, 30 s at 200 m out, 15 s at 600 m out. Stepping back in resets the ramp. You really want to be inside.
Hot zonesTwo or three alive at a time, spawning at 1:00, 5:00 and 9:00 and living three to four minutes. Pay cash per second to everyone inside, linear falloff from center to edge. The pot grows the longer a zone lives.
EconomyEveryone drops with $800. Income is money piles, safes, kill drops (victim's unspent cash plus a $300 bounty) and hot-zone ticks. Money is the only loot in the world. Nothing persists between matches.
StoreA wrist device you can raise anywhere. Raising takes 1.5 s, lowers the weapon, still lets you walk. A purchase lands in your inventory only if there is a slot and the weight fits; otherwise it is refused and nothing is charged. Fixed catalog, fixed prices.
InventoryTwo primaries, sidearm, melee, four throwables, two gadgets, armor, helmet, backpack. Backpack tier sets a weight cap for consumables and ammo; carried weight slows stamina.
MovementStand, crouch-walk, prone crawl, walk, run, sprint, jump, vault or mantle up to 1.5 m, ladder climb, lean left and right, dive from sprint into prone.
StaminaA 100-point pool. Sprint −10/s, dive −25, jump −8, holding aim −2/s (sway rises under 30). Regenerates +15/s after a 1 s pause. Weight and armor tier scale the drain.
WeaponsVietnam era only. Real projectiles: gravity and drag, per-cartridge muzzle velocity, penetration with energy loss, ricochet at shallow angles, hit-zone multipliers, armor with durability.
VehiclesCivilian ground only. Sedan, pickup, scooter, motorcycle with sidecar, Lambro three-wheeler, tractor. Suggested: a sampan for the canals. Nothing flies.
Drop-inAn orbital reentry screen: pick a landing spot, ride the capsule down, freefall with steering, parachute automatically at 300 m or earlier by hand. It is a state machine, not orbital mechanics.
BotsServer-side utility AI with personality profiles. "Learning" means the server records real player heatmaps (drop picks, loot routes, engagement ranges, hot-zone timing) into a JSON that bots sample from. Difficulty knobs: reaction time, aim error, perception, decision noise.
Map2 km × 2 km on Terrain3D. Rice paddies, jungle hills, a rubber plantation, river and canals, a small town, five hamlets, three military outposts, a bridge and a tunnel network.
TestingGUT (Godot Unit Test). CLI: godot --headless -s addons/gut/gut_cmdln.gd -gexit. Tasks in tasks/<cat>/<cat>-<name>.md in the format the /tdd skill reads.

Architecture

One Godot project, three runtime roles. The same shared/ simulation code runs on the client for prediction and on the server for truth; the server alone decides damage, cash and inventory.

Client

  • UI: HUD, wrist store, minimap, server browser
  • Viewmodels, audio, input
  • Predict own movement, reconcile on snapshot
  • Draw tracers from server events

Shared

  • Player controller, stamina
  • Ballistics, hit zones, penetration
  • Inventory rules, zone math
  • Data loader for data/*.json

Server

  • Match loop, spawner, money spawner, hot zones
  • 200 ms hitbox history for lag compensation
  • Bots feed the same input path as humans
  • Telemetry recorder, registry heartbeat

Registry

  • Python stdlib http.server
  • POST /heartbeat every 10 s
  • GET /servers for the browser
  • Own container

client ──input {vector, look, buttons, tick} @30Hz──▶ server ──snapshot Δ @20Hz + last acked tick──▶ client
client ──fire {tick, origin, dir}──▶ server rewinds hitboxes 200 ms ──▶ projectile sim ──▶ damage, tracer event

Rules that every task inherits

  • The client sends only its input and explicit requests such as buy(item_id). Every economy and damage decision is made on the server.
  • Shared code is deterministic: fixed tick, seeded RNG only, no randf() in the simulation. Prediction and server must agree.
  • No weapon or item numbers in scripts. Everything lives in data/*.json behind a loader with a schema test.
  • Bots use the same player state and input path as humans. There is no bot-only movement or damage code, so netcode stays single-path and bots cannot cheat by construction.
  • One task is one revertible commit.

Docker

Stage one downloads the Godot headless binary and Linux export templates and runs the server-linux export. Stage two is debian:bookworm-slim with the binary and pack, entrypoint ./server.x86_64 --headless -- --port=7777 --registry=http://registry:8080. Compose brings up the registry and one or two game servers on mapped UDP ports.

Design spec

Match flow

  1. lobby
    Server browser → join → team select → readyCountdown starts at two players or on the host timer. Bots fill the remaining slots. Joining after the drop puts you in spectate until the next match.
  2. −0:20
    Orbit screenMap overview. The team leader picks the drop point; teammates cluster within 150 m. Twenty seconds.
  3. 0:00
    Reentry → freefall → chuteFive seconds of no control in the capsule, then freefall at up to 60 m/s with 15 m/s of lateral steer. The chute opens itself at 300 m or by hand any time above that for a glide.
  4. 0:00–15:00
    The matchCircle shrinks from 1200 m to zero in a straight line. Center wanders. Hot zones open at 1:00, 5:00 and 9:00.
  5. end
    Last team aliveAfter 15:00 the circle is gone; the outside-damage ramp ends the match within about a minute. Scoreboard: kills, cash earned, cash spent, distance, seconds in hot zones.
  6. loop
    Back to the lobby on the same serverNext match starts from step one.

Economy

Cash is a number on the player. It is the only thing you can pick up and the only thing you can drop. It never decides a match; only survival does.

Ballistics server-side, shared code

Every shot is a projectile stepped once per server tick as a raycast segment, with gravity and quadratic drag. Damage comes from remaining energy, not a flat number per gun.

  • Hit zones: head 2.4, neck 1.5, chest 1.0, stomach 0.9, limbs 0.6.
  • Armor: vest and helmet reduce by tier percentage and lose durability per hit. A tier II helmet stops pistol rounds outright.
  • Penetration: wood 0.6, bamboo 0.7, sheet metal 0.4, brick 0.1, concrete 0, multiplied by remaining energy. One wall maximum.
  • Ricochet: hard surface, incidence under 15°, 30% chance, keeps 40% of energy.
  • Per weapon: RPM, fire modes, recoil pattern (vertical climb plus seeded horizontal), sway from stance, stamina and weight, spread for hip, aim and moving.
  • Reload: magazines are objects. Partial mags are kept, chamber holds +1, ammo types matter.
  • Attachments: era-correct scopes, rare suppressors, bipod, drum, foregrip, bayonet.
  • Feel: tracers every fifth round on LMGs; supersonic crack first, report delayed by distance at 343 m/s.

Movement & stamina

  • Stand, crouch-walk, prone crawl, walk, run, sprint.
  • Jump, vault or mantle ledges up to 1.5 m, ladders.
  • Lean left and right; leaning blocks sprint.
  • Dive: sprint into prone, 0.3 s of no aim.
  • Swim in canals: slow, weapons holstered, drop the pack to go faster.
  • Stamina 100. Sprint −10/s, dive −25, jump −8, aim hold −2/s.
  • Regen +15/s after 1 s without spending.
  • Under 30 stamina, weapon sway grows.
  • Weight and armor tier multiply every drain.
  • Prone in paddy water is invisible to bots beyond 40 m.

Bots server-only, same input path as humans

A utility scorer picks between loot, move to zone, go to hot zone, engage, flank, retreat and heal, buy, drive, camp, revive. Five personality profiles weight those scores: Rusher, Looter, Camper, Opportunist and Squad-follower.

  • Perception: 110° cone with occlusion raycasts; hears gunshots at 600 m, vehicles at 250 m, footsteps at 20 m.
  • Aim: target point plus angular error that shrinks with time on target; reaction 250–700 ms by difficulty; leads moving targets.
  • Navigation: baked navmesh with links for vault, ladder and tunnel entrances; road spline graph for driving.
  • Learning: every 2 s the server appends positions, loot events, engagement distances, drop picks and purchases to a JSONL file. An aggregator turns that into a 64×64 heat grid, loot-route popularity and buy priorities. Bots sample from those with a little noise.
  • Knobs: reaction time, aim error, perception range, decision noise.
  • Human things: they buy from the wrist store, drive, throw grenades, revive, and are worth spectating.

World

A 2048 m Terrain3D heightmap with hand-painted biomes and instanced foliage. Modular kits for stilt houses, huts, market stalls, a temple, colonial blocks, bunkers, sandbags, watchtowers, a bridge, tunnel segments, fences and paddy dikes.

  • Rice paddies: flooded, slow, hide prone players.
  • Jungle hills and a rubber plantation in a planted grid.
  • River and canals with swim volumes; one bridge.
  • Small town: market, temple, colonial blocks three to four floors high, safes.
  • Five hamlets of stilt houses.
  • Three military outposts with the richest piles.
  • A Cu-Chi-style tunnel network as flank routes.
  • Day and dusk first; monsoon rain later, masking sound and dulling bot perception.

UI

  • HUD: health, stamina, cash, compass strip, ammo, zone timer, kill feed, team panel, knocked indicators.
  • Maps: rotating minimap top-right; full map on M with the circle, the wander preview, hot zones, pings and bounty markers.
  • Wrist store: a 3D wrist device with a viewport UI: category tabs, item cards, a "fits" check, confirmation.
  • Inventory: slots, weight bar, drag and drop, attachments.
  • Server browser: list from the registry, filters for mode and ping, refresh, direct connect.
  • Around the match: lobby, team select, orbit pick, spectate teammates, end scoreboard.

What was missing from the brief all added to the task board

  1. Sound propagation. The single biggest thing for a first-person game: gunshot falloff by distance, supersonic crack, footsteps by surface, vehicle noise, rain masking.
  2. Knock-down and revive in team modes, plus spectating.
  3. Ping system so teams work without voice.
  4. Tunnels and booby traps. Cheap area denial, strong Vietnam flavour.
  5. Smuggler truck event instead of an airdrop: a civilian truck drives a route carrying $2500. Hijack it or blow it up.
  6. Rich bounty marker above $3000, so the economy keeps cycling through the store.
  7. Radio gadget that reveals the zone's next wander: information as an item.
  8. Weather and time variants that change bot perception and sound.
  9. Swimming rules for the canals.
  10. Anti-cheat basics: server authority, input rate limits, speed and teleport sanity, fire-rate checks, a per-match seed.
  11. Kill cam and match stats from the server's own history buffer.
  12. Settings and accessibility: rebinds, field of view, sensitivity, colourblind minimap.
  13. Deterministic loot layout from the match seed, so replays and bug reports reproduce.
  14. Telemetry for tuning prices and bot difficulty, using the same recorder the bots learn from.
